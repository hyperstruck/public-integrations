**declared_sensitivity** | **object** | Optional caller-declared metadata for this item, in the same vocabulary as a step&#x27;s. Nest these members under a &#x60;provenance&#x60; key, e.g. &#x60;{\&quot;provenance\&quot;: {\&quot;source_id\&quot;: \&quot;...\&quot;, \&quot;channel\&quot;: \&quot;...\&quot;}}&#x60;; a flat top-level &#x60;source_id&#x60; (or &#x60;channel&#x60;, &#x60;genre&#x60;, &#x60;author&#x60;, &#x60;source_time&#x60;, &#x60;source_class&#x60;) is refused rather than silently read as no declaration. The record may also carry a citation into a stored document: &#x60;doc_id&#x60;, &#x60;text_sha256&#x60; and &#x60;segmenter_version&#x60; as strings, and &#x60;unit_index&#x60;, &#x60;start&#x60; and &#x60;end&#x60; as INTEGERS, naming the exact passage this item&#x27;s evidence was drawn from. A numeric string for an offset is refused deliberately, because it is a second shape for the same pointer. &#x60;attacker_reachable&#x60; is a BOOLEAN: &#x60;true&#x60; says someone outside your organisation can write into this source, and holds what is read from it as untrusted. &#x60;false&#x60; is ignored, because a source vouching for itself is not believed. The &#x60;provenance&#x60; record says where the item came from, in two independent facets plus identity: &#x60;channel&#x60; is how it reached you (chat, email, meeting, document_store, system_of_record, api) and conditions how fast it goes stale; &#x60;genre&#x60; is what kind of artefact it is (policy, plan, minutes, reference, correspondence, note, aside) and conditions how authoritative it is. A signed policy pasted into a chat thread is both, which is why they are separate. Also &#x60;source_id&#x60;, &#x60;author&#x60;, and an RFC 3339 &#x60;source_time&#x60; for when the facts in it became true. An unrecognised &#x60;channel&#x60; or &#x60;genre&#x60; is kept verbatim and left unranked rather than refused. &#x60;source_class&#x60; is the superseded single-facet form, still accepted and never ranked. To say what the item is *about*, use the &#x60;subject&#x60; field above rather than this record. | [optional]

**source_ref** | **object** | Non-secret provenance: a doc id or URL. This is the input that decides whether a claim distilled from this item can name where it came from. A claim grounded in a passage of this item carries this string back as its citation&#x27;s &#x60;grounding_ref&#x60;, so a later question read answers with the filename you sent. Omit it and the claim is still recorded and still returned, but with &#x60;citation_absence: source_undeclared&#x60; instead of a citation, and the name cannot be recovered afterwards because it was never sent. **status** | **object** |  | [optional]
**subject** | **object** | The entity this item is about, e.g. an account or product name. Given, it is the name **every** fact from this item is filed under: it outranks the entity extraction reads out of the prose, so a fact about a person named inside the item is filed under this subject too. That is what stops one entity fragmenting across two spellings and never accumulating corroboration, and it is why the field suits an item your connector already knows is about exactly one thing, such as an account note matched to a CRM record. Omit it for an item covering several entities, a meeting transcript most of all, and each fact resolves its own entity from the prose. Sending the item&#x27;s title here files the whole meeting under the meeting. To say where an item came from rather than what it is about, use &#x60;source_ref&#x60; or the provenance record. | [optional]
